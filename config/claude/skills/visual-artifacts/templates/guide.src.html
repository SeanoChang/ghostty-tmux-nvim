Work Queues

A work queue lets producers hand off jobs and keep going. Workers take the jobs at their own pace, so a slow job never blocks a caller.

Math

Waiting callers grow with job time

At 100 requests a second and 2 s per job, 200 callers wait at once.

Little's law says the count in the system L is the arrival rate \lambda times the time each one stays, W.

L = \lambda W

Substitute the arrival rate and the job time.

L = 100\,\text{s}^{-1} \times 2\,\text{s}

The units cancel, so L is a plain count.

L = 200
Code

A queue with two slots

The queue holds jobs until a worker is free. A full queue refuses new jobs, and that refusal is backpressure.

Trade-offs

What the queue costs

You trade an immediate answer for throughput. You must also handle jobs that fail after you have returned.
